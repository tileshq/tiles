# ADR-070: The pane gets the house polish: every line on the same edges, a page of the theme's colour, a coloured status line, a raised tooltip, and a click that focuses its value instead of filtering

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-27
- Amends: [ADR-065](ADR-065-pane-ships-with-fetched-rows-and-one-interaction-state.md), whose click on a mark filtered the other cards' rows

## Context

After the Apple Silicon gate run on 26 November 2026 the owner said of the pane: "cool. the pane wasn't fancy and didn't have our design polish though", and later "in the pane tiles the top 3 scorecards aren't aligned to page width i think". The scorecards stop short because the dashboard planner caps a scorecard among other cards at its form's narrowest width (`Painter::fit`) and fits each line only to the widest line's asked width. On a 189-column window the three scorecards covered 107 columns under chart lines of 181. Around the cards the terminal's own background showed, the status line was plain text, and the hover drew a square white outline and a tooltip that could cover the card's title or the bar's value label.

The filter from ADR-065 had a second problem the mockups showed. Filtering the other cards to one region leaves a column chart with one giant column, a lollipop with one dot and a stacked bar with one stack, under titles such as "Germany places the most orders" that no longer describe what they draw.

On 27 November 2026 the owner saw static mockups of the pane: full width, half width, a hover, a click that focuses, and today's filter for comparison. The owner chose the focus option as drawn ("panes look good").

## Decision

### Layout

The pane lays its cards out with `Call::lay_out_pane`. It plans the rows as the chart tool does (`board::plan_full`), but over the window's width less `PANE_MARGIN`, two columns each side. It fits every line to that whole width: scorecards and slope charts stretch like the other charts, so every line shares the same outer edges and gutters at any width. Three scorecards that don't fit side by side split two and one as before, and each line still spans the width. Inline dashboards keep `board::plan` and every golden stays as it was.

### Page and heading

The theme's page colour, placed with `kitty::fill` stretched over the whole window at stacking order -1,073,741,825, sits under the text, under cells with a background colour such as the status line's key caps, and under the cards. At -1 the key caps' backgrounds vanished under it in Ghostty, as the kitty protocol has it. The pane sends it only with the first frame and after a resize. It sends three by three pixels and places only the middle one: kitty 0.51 scales a placement with linear filtering against a transparent border, so a lone stretched pixel faded towards the window's edges, which on the light theme left the heading and the status line on a dark rim. The colour is the theme's new `page` token (plain dark `#171614`, plain light `#f1eee7`). A design pack without one takes the card colour a third of the way to black on a dark theme and a twentieth on a light one, so design pack 1 still loads. The heading is `Engine::pane_heading`, the card heading on the cards' left edge. With a value focused, a chip reads `column value ×`, then the `r` key that clears it. Where the title keeps at least 60% of the heading's width beside the widest chip (the column cut to a twelfth of the width, the value to a seventh), the chip sits at the right of the title line and the title wraps short of it. On a narrower pane the chip takes its own line under the title, and the title keeps the full width. The heading leaves the chip's room from the first frame whether a value is focused or not, so focusing one never moves the cards or rewraps the title. The note keeps the full width.

### Status line

`tiles_term::pane::status_spans` builds the status line from coloured runs, each through `sanitize::run`. On the left it shows `tiles pane`, the call's name, then either what the user picked (the focused value, how many cards focus it, drills, hidden series, sorts) or a hint, then any note. The keys `q r s d u` sit at the right as key caps. The keys drop when the line is too narrow for both. The last column stays empty so the line never wraps. The only control sequences are the cursor moves, the erase and each run's own colour, reset after it, which the hostile corpus test checks.

### Hover

The overlay lightens the hovered mark with a wash of white at 12% opacity in the mark's shape, on every theme. The wash is translucent, so whatever the card draws over the mark shows through it: a box plot's median, a range band's inner band and middle dot, a heatmap cell's lift. Where a row's marks meet, directly or through one another, they get one ring and one wash, whichever of them the pointer is on. When every one is a dot, they go round the largest dot, the one under the pointer among equals, so a named point's larger dot stands for its smaller one. A drawn dot stands before the undrawn point that answers hover at the same place, so a small-multiples panel's last point takes the wash. Otherwise they go round one rectangle holding them all, rounded by the largest radius among them and square only where every one is square, so a range band's inner band and middle dot share the outer band's ring even where a narrow inner band, widened to a pill, sticks out of it. A row's marks that sit apart keep a ring each, so the ring stays slim on the mark the pointer is on: a big number's target bar and its sparkline's last point, a box and its low outlier, whose tooltip swatch takes the outlier's own colour, and a dumbbell's or slope chart's two end dots. It rings the mark with a one-pixel line in the accent, two pixels off its edge and following its shape: a circle round a dot, and round a bar a rectangle whose rounded corners are the bar's grown by the gap and whose square baseline corners take the gap's radius alone. The focus ring follows the same shape and the same rule, so a focused point or band never shows a doubled outline and a focused dumbbell or slope row rings each of its end dots. The ring is at 60% opacity, or the full accent on the focused value, and no overlay draws a stroke in the ink. Any value label the ring reaches is drawn again above it, so a ring never cuts a label. The first build drew a two-pixel ink ring inside a card-coloured halo; on the tall Germany column it cut the 6,900 label in half, and the owner asked of it, "not sure if i like such a huge white line? does it match our craft and polish?" A raised tooltip (`Palette::raised`, a hairline, a soft shadow) shows a swatch in the mark's colour and the mark's name, a rule, each other `name: value` line as a muted name and a value on the right, and a hint for the click. The hint reads "click to focus France", or "click again to clear" on the focused value. The tooltip never covers the card's title or any value label, and `pane::place_tip` tries four places in order. First it goes below the mark, starting just left of the pointer and sliding along the row until it is clear. Next it goes beside the mark, on the side with more room, within the plot's width, moving up or down until it is clear, from the plot's top down to the category labels under the plot, which it may cover. A tall column at full width lands here, beside its column and under the next column's value label; at half width, where the shorter columns' value labels leave no clear band inside the plot, it sits lower, over the category labels. Then it goes inside the mark's upper part, when the mark is wider than the tooltip. Last it takes the clear place anywhere in the tile nearest the spot just below the mark, trying only places whose left and top edges sit at the tile's edge or just past a title's or value label's box; a middle column at half width, with no room below or beside it, lands low over the category labels, and a bar or lollipop mark at half width lands clear of the other rows' value labels. Of those places it prefers one that also leaves the ring clear. A ring no bigger than 24 pixels a side, a point's, which the tooltip could hide whole, it keeps clear even at the cost of a label, so on a scorecard the tooltip for the sparkline's last point may cover the change line but never the point. The live drive of 27 November found the tooltip drawn over that point on every dashboard scorecard. When no place is clear, it falls back to below the mark, or above it when the tile has no room below, or beside the mark, on the side with more room, when the tile is shorter than the tooltip. The ring and the tooltip go as two tight pictures (`pane::Overlay`'s `ring` and `tip`, image ids 6999990 or 6999991 and two more), so the empty space between a wide bar and a tooltip under it is neither drawn nor sent. No card is sent again on hover.

### Click

A click on a mark focuses its value (`State::focus`): every card keeps all its rows, and each card whose form highlights by the selected column and holds the value highlights it in the accent, whatever the card's claim tone. The form-aware check from PR #15 decides which cards can, so a stacked bar, which highlights only by its series, keeps its own colours when a region is clicked. A second click on the focused value, on whichever card holds it, a click on empty space, or `r` clears the focus. The pane still lays out and sends only the cards whose scene changed, and the heading when its chip changes. A focused card shows its focused mark in the accent with the same one-pixel accent ring, and moves its other marks 30% of the way to the card colour (`Engine::pane_focus`). The pane applies it to every card whose highlight names the focused value, the cards that already named it by their own claim too, so a focus redraws those cards as well.

## Consequences

- A click no longer narrows the other cards. A user who wants one region's rows alone asks the agent for that chart.
- A focus redraws the cards whose highlight changes and the heading. On the fixture a click on Germany sends five pictures (the bar, the columns, the lines, the lollipop and the heading) where the filter sent four.
- The pane's colours come from the theme tokens, so a release build draws the house theme and a build from source draws the plain one. The design pack takes the `page` token when its repository next changes.
- The tooltip carries more than before: a swatch, a rule, a hint and a shadow. Drawn as one picture with the ring, its hover took about three times as long on the 2017 Intel Mac and sent twice the bytes, which is why the ring and the tooltip go as two pictures. The gate numbers in the pull request show the result.
