# ADR-044: On the pre-cut route the whole card shows from its block's first row, a replaced picture goes a tick later, and each session numbers its pictures apart

- Status: Accepted; its "a block with no room waits to be whole" is amended by [ADR-053](ADR-053-precut-entrance-waits-for-a-still-card.md): a card that waited for room and has an entrance to play goes up once it stops moving, not as soon as it has room; its replaced picture going a tick later covers a resize too, by [ADR-057](ADR-057-precut-fence-resize-and-waiting-rows.md)
- Date: 2026-10-20
- Supersedes: [ADR-030](ADR-030-entrance-by-the-clock-on-every-route.md)'s "the card grows with its block" (each paint placing a strip for the rows typed since the last) and its "new placed before old deleted" in one update; [ADR-017](ADR-017-early-card-and-entrance.md)'s consequence that Warp keeps ADR-011's rule because nothing there was driven with early placement

## Context

The owner tested v0.1.9 in Warp on a laptop on 2026-10-20 in Claude Code, Pi and OpenCode. Every card and dashboard came in "loading downwards like an old internet explorer page", with "no animation, no smoothness or polish"; dark mode showed "a glitch happening every ms", also with `TILES_PRECUT_SEND=tick`; OpenCode's dashboard was "breaking between loading"; and a light card had a line through it. His bar, set on 2026-10-17, is a card that arrives whole with a natural flow in, never revealed row by row.

The diagnosis (from his recordings, the session trace and probes in Warp v0.2026.10.07.08.29 on the development Mac) found:

- **The reveal.** The model types the placeholder block, one `[tile:…]` line per card row, at its own output rate: a row every 100 to 150 ms in Claude Code and OpenCode, 15 to 50 ms in Pi. On the pre-cut route the card showed only the rows typed so far, so a line card took 0.8 to 2.1 s and a dashboard 1.3 to 3.4 s to come in, a band at a time. The 0.45 s entrance had ended by the time 5 to 17 rows existed, so it played on a strip at the top. Kitty has placed the whole card on the first row since ADR-017; Warp never did.
- **The flicker.** Each entrance frame and each move placed a new picture and deleted the one before in the same synchronized update. Replaying that in Warp left the card missing for 33 to 100 ms after some swaps (23 of 131 frames at 60 fps); each card was replaced 15 to 47 times on its way in. `TILES_PRECUT_SEND=tick` changes only when frames are sent. Deleting each replaced picture one step later left no frame without the card (0 of 312).
- **Pictures from other sessions.** Every `tiles run` numbered its pictures from `0x500000`, so a session reused the ids of the cards an earlier session had left in the same tab's scrollback. In Warp, sending an id again repaints every placement of that id: an earlier session's card in scrollback turned into the new session's strip, stretched to the old card's rows. The OpenCode dashboard showed Pi's line card and an earlier dashboard's title for single frames while it came in.
- **Seams.** A card that grew as strips stayed as strips once whole; the light card's stray line sat on a boundary between two of them. That a Warp gap between strips causes it is not proved: strips of 32 and 31 pixels showed no seam on the development Mac, whose Warp cell is about 28.8 pixels tall.

## Decision

- **The whole card from the first row.** On `Route::KittyPrecut` a block still being typed reaches past its typed rows to every row it will take (`Painter::growing`). Its first row places the whole card as one picture; the rows typed under it place nothing. The entrance plays on that whole card from its first showing, frame by frame as ADR-030 set, and the crisp card ends it. A block whose first rows scroll away is followed as before, and a card still being typed is placed again for a move at most once a tick.
- **Rows not typed yet are blanked.** The rows the card covers that its block has not reached yet hold the harness's own lines (Pi's editor, OpenCode's "Build" line). They are blanked like tokens, on the background beside the block, so nothing shows through the card's margins or its fading frames; the painter remembers them and writes the harness's text back once the card leaves those rows. Only the typed rows bound the card's width, so a line under the block never narrows it. The card covers the harness's lines under it while the block is typed, 0.8 to 3.4 s; it does not stop at the harness's footer as the kitty route's backing does, since Pi's editor sits right under the typed row and stopping there grew the card a row at a time again.
- **A block with no room waits to be whole.** A block whose first row has fewer rows under it, down to the screen's last, than the block will take places nothing: its tokens are blanked as they are typed. Its card goes up whole, with its entrance, once the screen has room for every row (the reply has scrolled the block up far enough) or once every row has been typed, whichever comes first; a card whose block's first rows have scrolled off by then shows its visible rows. A block that has room keeps its card from the first row.
- **The reach ends when typing stops.** The reach past the typed rows ends once the block has gone 1.5 s without a new row (`STALL`), and on nothing else: what the harness writes under the typed rows (OpenCode's ticking "Build 12.4s" timer, a spinner, the next row half typed, Claude Code's "Interrupted" line) does not end it. The card is then cut to the typed rows and the harness's lines under them are written back and no longer blanked. Only a new row restarts the reach, and the whole card goes up again; a resize, a switch to the alternate screen and back, or the harness drawing the typed rows again a few at a time keeps a stalled block cut.
- **A replaced picture goes a tick later.** A picture placed over another (a moved card, a cut, the next entrance frame, the crisp card) goes up at a higher stacking order (`z`), and the one it replaces is deleted at the first tick at least 50 ms later (`Painter::retire`), never in the update that places its replacement. A card that leaves the screen, a resize and a switch of screen still delete at once, since nothing replaces them.
- **Each session numbers its pictures apart.** On the pre-cut route the painter's own image ids start at a point drawn from the session's token, in the 32-bit range above every card's own 24-bit id (`0x0100_0000` to `0xffff_fffe`), and wrap within it. A session uses a few thousand ids, so two sessions in one tab share none in practice. Kitty keeps its ids.
- **A whole card is one picture.** A whole card in several strips, or about to gain one, goes up again as one picture once its entrance is over.

## Consequences

- In Pi, and wherever a block is typed near the screen's bottom, the card covers the harness's editor or status lines until the reply moves them below it, or until typing stops. A user typing into the harness meanwhile does not see the letters under the card until then.
- A reply the user interrupts, or a stream the proxy drops, leaves a card cut to the rows typed 1.5 s after the last row; until then the card covers the harness's Interrupted line and input box, and after it they and the next prompt show under it. A pause of 1.5 s or more in the middle of a block cuts the card the same way, and the next row puts the whole card back.
- A block typed near the bottom of a full screen, Claude Code's usual case after a few exchanges, shows nothing where its tokens were until the reply has scrolled it up far enough for the whole card, or until it is whole: for a 23-row card typed above a five-row input box, its 18th row. A block taller than the screen shows its card only when whole. The card never comes in a band at a time.
- A moved card leaves its previous copy one tick under the new one, at most 50 to 100 ms, visible only at the edge the move uncovered.
- Whether the strips' seam was Warp's is still open; merging the strips removes it either way. The laptop check looks for it.
- None of this has been seen in Warp on the laptop: the paint tests, the Warp pseudo-terminal tests and the probes on the development Mac are the proof until the owner's next recording.
